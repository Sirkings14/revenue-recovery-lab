# Sample Lead Response & Follow-Up Audit
## Illustrative example — fictional company and synthetic data only

**Purpose:** Demonstrate the format and level of detail delivered by Revenue Recovery Lab.
**Important:** This is not an audit of a real company. The company, journey, events, timings, and figures below are fictional and must not be presented as measured client results.

## 1. Scope

Illustrative business: Northstar Industrial Supplies (fictional B2B distributor)

Journey reviewed: Website quote request → sales assignment → first response → next-step follow-up.

Evidence supplied: Synthetic process description only. No real customer records, inboxes, CRM data, or system access.

## 2. Example journey map

| Stage | Illustrative event | Expected owner | Control question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enquiry submitted | Website quote form is completed | Sales coordinator | Is submission acknowledged and logged? |
| Assignment | Enquiry is routed to a product team | Sales manager | Is one named owner accountable? |
| First response | Owner contacts the prospect | Assigned salesperson | Is a response target defined and measured? |
| Qualification | Requirements and buying timeline are clarified | Assigned salesperson | Is the next action recorded? |
| Follow-up | Quote or information is sent | Assigned salesperson | Is a dated next step scheduled? |
| Closure / nurture | Opportunity progresses or enters nurture | Sales manager | Is the outcome and reason recorded? |

## 3. Illustrative observations

These are example control gaps in a fictional process, not claims about any real business.

| Priority | Example observation | Why it matters | Suggested correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | No explicit backup owner when the assigned salesperson is unavailable | Enquiries may remain unowned during absence | Define a backup queue and escalation owner |
| P1 | No required next-action date after a quote is sent | Follow-up can depend on individual memory | Require a dated next action before the record can leave the active stage |
| P2 | First-response target is not defined | The team cannot consistently assess response performance | Agree a realistic service-level target and measure it |
| P2 | No standard reason code for closed-lost or unqualified enquiries | Management cannot distinguish process outcomes | Add a small, consistent outcome taxonomy |

## 4. Recommended operating workflow

1. **Trigger:** A new quote enquiry is submitted.
2. **Ownership:** Assign one primary owner and one backup queue.
3. **Acknowledgement:** Confirm receipt automatically without promising a delivery date.
4. **First action:** The owner reviews the request and records the first meaningful response.
5. **Next step:** Every active enquiry receives an owner, next action, and due date.
6. **Escalation:** If the agreed response target is missed, notify the backup owner or sales manager.
7. **Closure:** Record outcome and reason; route appropriate prospects into a consent-compliant nurture process.

## 5. 14-day measurement plan

| Metric | Definition | Review |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiry ownership coverage | Share of new enquiries with a named owner | Daily |
| First-response time | Time from submission to first meaningful human response | Daily / weekly |
| Overdue next actions | Active enquiries with a missed next-action date | Daily |
| Follow-up completion | Due follow-ups completed within the agreed window | Weekly |
| Outcome completeness | Closed enquiries with a recorded outcome/reason | Weekly |

**Day 1–2:** Agree definitions, owners, and baseline.
**Day 3–7:** Record activity without changing several controls at once.
**Day 8:** Review bottlenecks and adjust one or two process controls.
**Day 9–14:** Continue measurement and compare against the baseline.

No target or improvement percentage is assumed in this sample. Targets should be agreed with the client after the baseline is understood.
