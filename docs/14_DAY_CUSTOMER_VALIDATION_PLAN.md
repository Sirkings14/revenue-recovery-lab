# Revenue Recovery Lab — 14-Day Customer Validation Plan

## Objective

Validate a narrowly defined service with real prospects before building more software. The long-term goal is to work toward **$200–$300/day in average gross revenue** (about $6,000–$9,000/month). This is a business target, not a forecast or guarantee. Revenue, costs, taxes, payment fees and delivery capacity must be tracked separately.

## First offer

### Lead Response & Follow-Up Recovery Sprint

**Who it is for:** small marketing agencies and other B2B service firms that receive enquiries through their website, forms, email or calendar but do not have a consistently managed follow-up process.

**Problem:** enquiries can be missed, answered slowly, left without a next step, or lost because ownership and follow-up are unclear.

**What the client receives:**
1. A short intake call or questionnaire about lead sources and current follow-up.
2. A review of the public enquiry journey and any client-provided, non-sensitive workflow screenshots or exports.
3. A concise findings report: observed friction, evidence, business risk, and prioritized fixes.
4. A simple follow-up workflow map with owner, trigger, timing and next action.
5. One agreed implementation recommendation or a small, scoped automation setup.
6. A handover checklist and a 14-day measurement plan.

**Scope boundary:** do not request passwords, API keys, payment details, private customer records, or unnecessary personal data. Use synthetic or redacted examples. Do not claim recovered revenue unless the client supplies verifiable before/after data.

## Pricing experiments

These are test prices, not established market rates. Quote in USD and confirm payment method, scope and delivery time before starting.

- **Pilot audit:** $99–$149 fixed fee for one defined lead journey and a short findings report.
- **Implementation sprint:** $350–$750 after the audit, depending on integrations and complexity.
- **Ongoing optimization:** $300–$800/month for agreed monitoring, workflow improvements and reporting.

For the first two clients, prioritize a clear paid pilot and a usable testimonial or permission to publish an anonymized case study. Do not give away full implementation for free. Any discount must have a defined scope and end date.

## Ideal prospect signals

Prioritize businesses that:
- sell a service worth at least several hundred dollars;
- visibly invite enquiries, quote requests, consultations or demos;
- have a real website and identifiable business contact;
- show a plausible follow-up gap that can be described specifically;
- appear small enough for the owner or sales lead to make a decision quickly.

Avoid mass scraping, generic bulk email, misleading subject lines, fabricated urgency, and repeated follow-ups after an opt-out. Use publicly listed business contact channels, personalize every first message, respect applicable privacy and marketing rules, and stop when asked.

## 14-day validation sequence

### Days 1–2: Make the offer credible
- Finalize the one-page offer and a sample report using a fictional business clearly labelled as a demonstration.
- Define the intake questions, report template, delivery checklist and boundaries.
- Check that the website contact route and business email actually work before using them.

### Days 3–5: Build a small qualified prospect list
- Choose one niche: small marketing agencies first.
- Manually qualify 30 businesses against the ideal-prospect signals.
- Record company, website, public contact route, one observed issue, source URL, date checked, outreach status and next action.
- Do not automate sending yet.

### Days 6–10: Personalized outreach
- Send 5–8 carefully researched messages per working day, within the recipient's rules and local law.
- Each message should mention one verifiable observation, explain the possible operational consequence without overstating it, and ask permission to send a short review.
- Follow up once after 4–5 business days if there is no reply; then stop.
- Track positive replies, calls booked, paid pilots and objections.

### Days 11–14: Deliver and decide
- Aim to close and deliver one paid pilot; do not treat this as guaranteed.
- Ask what was useful, what was unclear, and whether the client wants implementation.
- Review conversion data and revise the niche, offer or price based on evidence.
- If 30 well-qualified prospects produce no meaningful interest, change the message or niche before increasing volume.

## Daily operating block (2–4 hours)

1. 45–60 min: research and qualify 5–8 new prospects.
2. 30–45 min: write and send personalized outreach; update the pipeline.
3. 45–90 min: build sample audits or deliver paid client work.
4. 15 min: review metrics and schedule the next action.

## Pipeline fields

Use these stages: New → Qualified → Contacted → Replied → Call booked → Pilot proposed → Paid → Delivery → Implementation offered → Retainer → Closed / Not a fit.

Track weekly:
- qualified prospects added;
- personalized messages sent;
- positive replies;
- calls booked and attended;
- paid pilots;
- revenue collected;
- delivery hours and direct costs;
- implementation conversion and recurring revenue.

The first validation milestone is **one paid pilot**, followed by two more clients with repeatable delivery. Only after the offer converts should prospect research, report generation, CRM updates and follow-up reminders be automated.

## Revenue path (illustrative, not a promise)

A possible later mix could be 6 clients paying $750/month plus 3 implementation sprints at $500 in a month = $6,000 gross revenue for that month. This is not $200/day profit: delivery capacity, client churn, acquisition costs, payment fees, taxes and uneven payment timing all matter. Build toward repeatable monthly revenue rather than assuming a fixed daily payout.

## Rules for automation

Automate research assistance, report formatting, reminders and pipeline hygiene first. Keep prospect approval, message review, pricing, client commitments and any changes to client systems under human review. Never send bulk or unreviewed outreach automatically. Never claim an audit or automation has produced results that have not been measured.
